1.  How does a first negative hyperlink shape the relationship between two subreddits?  
    After a first negative hyperlink, does the number of hyperlinks between the two communities increase or decrease, and in which direction? Does the attacked community respond, or does it withdraw and interact mainly with similar subreddits? Does the impact depend on whether the link is in the title or the body? Which subreddits attack most, which are targeted most often, and which counterattack? Does the topic of the communities play a role? Do positive first links strengthen relationships? I would identify the first link between pairs of subreddits, classify it as negative or positive, and compare hyperlink volume, labels and negativity (measured by VADER compound, LIWC Anger and Dissent) in both directions before and after it. I would also use the embeddings to test whether attacked communities shift their links toward similar subreddits. To see if the topic plays a role, I would rank subreddits by attacks sent, received and returned, and see if the main subreddits speak about similar topics.

2.  Impact of political events on subreddit communities  
    Do major political events (Paris climate agreement, Brexit, 2016 US presidential election) increase activity and negativity between related subreddits? Do sensitive topics such as war, immigration or climate change have a bigger impact than others? Do these events polarize communities, meaning that negativity between left and right leaning subreddits increases more than within each side? Does the way an event is announced affect subreddit activity? I would build a list of events by topic and country (most important ones such as the USA, China, EU, UK) and compare hyperlink volume, share of negative hyperlinks, readability and LIWC Anger, Dissent, Social and Future around each event. I would classify subreddits as left or right wing by comparing their embeddings with political subreddits clearly on either side (like democrats, republicans or subreddits supporting politicians).

3.  Link between crypto markets and conflicts between crypto communities  
    How do the volume and label of hyperlinks between crypto subreddits change between high and low volatility market regimes? Do changes in interaction happen before or after market movements, and can they help predict them? Do subreddits of highly correlated coins have positive relationships and those of weakly correlated coins more hostile ones? I would identify the main crypto subreddits representing the most important coins (by market cap) and the crypto market in general, and measure hyperlink volume and share of negative hyperlinks per day or week. I can obtain daily price, volume and market cap for each coin from CoinGecko and define volatility regimes by identifying periods of large price swings. Then I would compare coin correlations to the label of hyperlinks between their communities.
