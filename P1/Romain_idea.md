# When Attention Turns into Conflict

## 1. Does reciprocity reduce hostility?

Reciprocal community pairs are 7.8% negative, compared with 10.5% for one-way relationships. We would examine whether ordinary mutual interaction is more civil than unilateral attention.

## 2. Does a negative beginning prevent reciprocity from developing?

Among relationships first observed before 2016, 13.4% of initially non-negative relationships eventually become reciprocal, compared with 9.0% of initially negative relationships. This suggests that hostile beginnings may be associated with relationships remaining one-sided.

## 3. When reciprocity develops, does initial negativity produce a hostile response?

After an initially non-negative link, the first reverse link is negative 4.5% of the time. After an initially negative link, the first reverse link is negative 11.8% of the time. Within seven days, the difference is 3.7% versus 14.0%.

## 4. Is reciprocal conflict mutual or mostly one-sided?

Among 3,410 reciprocal relationships with at least five links in each direction, and defining a direction as hostile when at least 20% of its links are negative:

- 2,928 relationships are non-hostile in both directions;
- 352 are hostile primarily in one direction;
- 130 are mutually hostile.

Among the hostile reciprocal relationships, approximately 73% are therefore one-sided.

## 5. Does negative attention spread across independent communities?

After one source links negatively to a target, the next link from another source within seven days is negative 16.8% of the time, compared with 9.9% otherwise. Comparing targets with their own usual behavior reduces the difference, but a median increase of approximately 2.3 percentage points remains across sufficiently active targets.
